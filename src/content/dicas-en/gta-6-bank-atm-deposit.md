Your GTA 6 bank account unlocks in **Chapter 1, during the mission Buck Me**. From then on you can deposit cash at **any ATM** on the map. Before that, the safe place for money is the stash at home.

According to Hazard, Buck Me shows up after the party Jason and his friends throw for Lucia at the **Rusty Anchor**, a couple of hours into the game at most. In it you open a checking account and make your first deposit at an ATM.

## Why you should stash cash

Early on, a few hundred dollars can make or break you, and the money in your wallet is never fully safe:

- **Dying or getting arrested** costs you. PC Gamer puts the penalty at a few hundred dollars. MrBossFTW and Vice City Dispatch describe something harsher: you drop **all the loose cash** you were carrying.
- **Strangers** on the street may try to rob you. Milo tested the rumor that locals mug people at ATMs and got held up **outside a store with no ATM at all**. It isn't just a bank thing, so don't carry more than you need.
- **There's a cap:** you can only carry up to **$20,000** on you. Mr Lawrence and Vice City Dispatch give the same number, so once you get close, go deposit.

So the habit to build is simple: stash often.

## Before the bank: the floorboard stash

For the first few chapters you have no account. You can still hide money **under the floorboards in the bedroom** of Jason's house in Key Lento. Whatever sits there **stays put** if you die or get busted.

More on that in [do you lose money when you die](../gta-6-lose-money-when-you-die/).

Hazard describes the stash as a hole under the floorboard with a lockbox and a pile of cash. He also flags a catch at this stage: **without an account, you can't pay for repairs** at a gas station or Pay 'n' Spray, because they only take BuckMe. Cash still works at grocery stores and for fuel. Other channels show gas station repairs without mentioning this, so treat it as his experience.

## Stolen goods go in the trunk

Jewelry, drugs and marked bills from robberies can sit in **your car's trunk**. Even if the cops impound the car, you can get the haul back later and sell it.

Just don't use a **car you don't own**. Lose the car and you lose what's inside. When it's time to sell, see the [fence locations](../gta-6-fence-locations/).

## How to deposit at an ATM

1. Play up to the mission **Buck Me** in Chapter 1. It opens your account.
2. On the map, look for the **hand with a credit card** icon. Those are ATMs. According to BennyCentral, the **BuckMe app on your phone** points you to the nearest one. Deposits only happen at an ATM, not from your phone.
3. Walk up to one and interact with it.
4. Choose to deposit.

### What the ATM screen looks like

Mr Lawrence recorded a deposit with no commentary at all, so everything here comes from what's on screen. Hover over the icon on the map and its label reads **ATM**; you can set it as your destination from there. He used one in Key Lento, mounted on the wall of a green wooden building.

- **The screen is called Your Account**, with your balance in the top right corner.
- **There are three options:** **Deposit**, **Withdraw** and **Transactions**, plus **Exit**. With a $0 balance in his video, Withdraw was grayed out.
- **PS5 buttons:** **Cross** selects, **Circle** backs out and **R2** zooms in on the screen.
- **Two balances on your HUD:** the top one, next to a small "b" logo, is your BuckMe account; the one underneath is the cash in your pocket. It's a quick way to check the deposit went through.

Having an account also **unlocks some weapons** for purchase. PC Gamer mentions an **interest-bearing savings account** too, but doesn't explain how it works.

## The BuckMe account

MrBossFTW explains what changes once the story opens your **BuckMe** account:

- the money in it can be used by **both Jason and Lucia**;
- it **isn't lost** when you die or get arrested;
- there's a **deposit limit**, and it goes **up** as you progress through the main story.

His routine: early on, keep most of your money in the safehouse stash. Once the account opens, grab the stash and deposit it into BuckMe.

## Account limits and BuckMe perks

Your account has a **deposit cap**. Near the end of Chapter 1, Elanip got an **"Account Limit $15,000"** notice on the ATM screen. Soon after, the account was full and the ATM wouldn't take any more, leaving him holding the cash while the cops closed in.

MrBossFTW says the cap **rises with the story**. Hazard explains it differently: BuckMe perks grow as you **save money**. By his account:

| How much you save | Account limit | Extra perk |
|---|---|---|
| $5,000 | $15,000 | |
| $10,000 | $100,000 | 20% off RideMe trips |
| $100,000 or more | $500,000 | 20% off garage purchases |

That table comes from Hazard's voiceover alone; the app screen isn't shown in his video. Still, the first tier matches the "Account Limit $15,000" Elanip saw. See [garage locations and prices](../gta-6-garage-locations-prices/).

## The couple's savings and the ending

Heads up: this part covers how the ending is decided, without giving away the story.

According to GameSpot, Jason and Lucia's **shared savings** max out at **$2 million**. Hitting that cap while their relationship is also maxed out is what earns the **best ending**. A maxed relationship without full savings gets you a good ending, but not the best one. The details are in [how many endings GTA 6 has](../gta-6-how-many-endings/).

In other words, what you save isn't just protection against losing cash. It counts toward the end of the game.

## Savings goals: two versions

Channels don't agree yet on how much the story asks you to save:

- **$5,000:** WiLLiSGaming says that early on Jason's credit is too bad for a normal bank account, so the bank gives you the BuckMe app instead and asks you to build up **$5,000** before the story moves on.
- **A goal per chapter:** Vice City Dispatch, which builds its videos from review excerpts, says BuckMe sets a savings target each chapter and rewards you for hitting it. One review it quotes describes a family savings account with targets starting at **$100,000**, then **$500,000**, and says those deposits can't be withdrawn.

Both could be true, with the bigger numbers coming in later chapters, but no video has shown those larger goals on screen yet. Note that Vice City Dispatch's $100,000 and $500,000 are the same figures Hazard gives as **account limits**, not goals. And GameSpot puts the cap on the couple's savings at **$2 million**. For now, follow whatever goal your game gives you. Also note the regular ATM does have a Withdraw option, so if that lock exists, it applies to the family savings goal, not your everyday account.

## Don't bank every dollar

This is the guide's key advice. Plenty of GTA 6's street moments only happen if you have **cash in hand**:

- a stranger selling secrets, each one pricier than the last;
- a **bribe** to get quick access to a restricted area;
- a **pool** hustler in a dive bar;
- **vendors** who only take cash.

So whenever you deposit, **keep a few hundred in your wallet**. That way you won't have to run off looking for an ATM in the middle of one of those encounters.
