GTA 6 runs at **30fps on every console**: PS5, PS5 Pro, Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S. There's no 60fps mode and no 40fps mode either. What changes from one console to the next is **how sharp the image looks** and **how well each one holds** that 30.

The numbers here come from Digital Foundry's two tech reviews of the final game, one covering PS5 and PS5 Pro and the other covering Xbox Series X and Series S.

## Every console at a glance

| Console | Internal resolution | How well it holds 30fps |
|---|---|---|
| PS5 Pro | 1440p, rebuilt with PSSR | Nearly flawless; 24fps at worst during heavy explosions |
| PS5 | 1440p | Small dips throughout; sustained drops in heavy chaos |
| Xbox Series X | About 1440p | Stable most of the time; drops in chases and shootouts, around 20fps at worst |
| Xbox Series S | 720p | Often in the mid to upper 20s; around 20fps at worst |
| Xbox One (cloud) | Uses Series S settings | Depends on your connection |

## Why there's no 60fps mode

Rockstar's technology production director told Kotaku that the PS5 Pro's processor is only slightly better than the base PS5's and **isn't powerful enough to run the game's simulation** at higher frame rates. Kotaku asked about a 60fps or 40fps option arriving later, and Rockstar said it won't happen on current consoles. Digital Foundry calls GTA 6 a 30fps-only game on console, at least at launch.

## PS5 Pro is the best way to play

PlayStation's official page lists two Pro upgrades: **PSSR upscaling** for extra clarity and **ray-traced shadows**. According to Digital Foundry, both PS5 models render at 1440p internally, but on Pro the PSSR output convincingly passes for 4K, while the base PS5 looks like a stretched 1440p image.

Digital Foundry's reviewer played more than 50 hours on Pro without spotting a single dip. He only got it to drop by filling a street with explosions, and even then it bottomed out at 24fps.

## Xbox Series X and Series S

On **Series X**, the game holds 30fps the vast majority of the time. Drops show up when the processor gets hammered: fast chases, crashes, or gunfights in crowded intersections. The worst Digital Foundry logged was around 20fps. Windows Central, which reviewed on Series X, says it didn't notice dips even at top speed on the freeway.

**Series S** keeps nearly every visual feature from Series X, just at lower quality and a 720p internal resolution. The cost is performance: combat and fast driving keep it in the mid to upper 20s for long stretches. Kotaku's launch coverage quoted Digital Foundry calling it the loosest 30fps lock of the four consoles, and Digital Foundry suggests playing on another console for now.

## Settings that help

There's no quality versus performance choice. These are the settings Digital Foundry flagged:

- **Motion blur:** goes from 0 to 10, with 3 as the default. Maxed out, motion looks smoother; turned off, the image is clearer but choppier.
- **Camera sensitivity:** the camera feels sluggish, and raising sensitivity helps a bit.
- **120Hz with VRR:** on Series X, enabling this on your TV smooths over dropped frames. It helps less on Series S.
- **Locked:** depth of field and the edge vignette can't be changed.
